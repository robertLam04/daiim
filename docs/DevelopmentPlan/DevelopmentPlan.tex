\section{Team Member Roles}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Agenda creator}: The agenda creator is responsible for creating
  the agenda for a meeting, and posting it to the meeting issue. The agenda creator will 
  use the template for team meetings located in the repo, containing the agenda and an attendance tracker. 
  
  \item \textbf{Notetaker}: The notetaker is responsible for taking notes during a meeting, and posting the meeting minutes to the meeting issue. 
  The meeting minutes will be posted as a comment under the meeting issue. 
  Multiple people may contribute to the notetaker role during a meeting, especially if there are multiple topics / individual updates from each person.
  
  \item \textbf{Pull request reviewer}:
  The pull request reviewer role is a role that is assigned to a team member for each pull request. The pull request reviewer is responsible for reviewing the code changes in the pull request, providing feedback, and approving or requesting changes to the pull request.
  Every team member is expected to review pull requests, but the pull request reviewer is the primary reviewer for that pull request. They are in charge of making the final decision on approving the request. 

  \item \textbf{Developer}: The developer role is a role assigned to a team member for each development task. The developer is responsible for implementing the task, and updating the associated issue with their progress.
  Every team member is expected to take on this role and contribute to development tasks as necessary, but the developer is the primary person responsible for completing a given task.
  
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Branches and Commits}

\textbf{Branch naming convention:} All work will be done on a new branch named \texttt{<branch-type>/<branch-name>}, where the branch name is a short kebab-case description of the work. For example, a branch for the issue ``Implement user login'' would be named \texttt{feat/implement-user-login}. Typically, a branch will be created for each issue, but a branch may close several issues if they are related and can be completed together. The branch types are the same as the first four commit types in Table~\ref{tab:commit-types}: \texttt{feat}, \texttt{fix}, \texttt{docs}, and \texttt{test}.

\textbf{Commit naming convention:} Each commit message will follow the format \texttt{<commit-type>: <commit-message>}, based on the \href{https://www.conventionalcommits.org/en/v1.0.0/}{Conventional Commits} specification. The message is written in lowercase and in the imperative mood, and describes what the commit changes. For example, a commit for the issue ``Implement user login'' would be named \texttt{feat: implement user login}. A commit that completes an issue references it in its body (e.g., \texttt{Closes \#42}). Commits made automatically by the CI pipeline, such as regenerated PDFs, are exempt from this convention.

\subsection{Pull Requests and Code Review}
A pull request will be created once the work for an issue is done. Its title follows the commit naming convention, and its description summarizes the changes and links the issue(s) it resolves with \texttt{Closes \#<issue-number>}, so that the issue closes when the pull request is merged. The author assigns a primary reviewer and moves the card to In Review. Before requesting review, the author merges the latest \texttt{main} into the branch and resolves any conflicts. The reviewer approves the pull request or requests changes, and gives at least one point of constructive feedback, as described in the team charter.

\subsection{Merging Strategy}
Developers will merge their changes into the main branch through pull requests.
A pull request must be approved by at least one other team member, and the CI checks (including the LaTeX build) must pass, before it can be merged into the main branch.
Pull requests will be merged with a merge commit, so that individual commits are preserved in the history.

\section{Proof of Concept Demonstration Plan}

\subsection{Project Risks}

One main risk is designing an aggregation algorithm that combines predictions
from multiple inference providers into an accurate final result. The algorithm
must handle disagreements between providers and reduce the influence of
dishonest or low-quality predictions. This is difficult because, for a given
task, the network may not have a source of \textit{ground truth} against which
to compare the predictions. There is also a cold-start problem where initially
there may not be enough trusted providers in the network to correctly evaluate
one another. 

Another risk is designing the incentive system and token economics so that the network
is sufficiently decentralized and is resistant to attacks. The system must be fair and accessible for 
participants so that they are encouraged to take part in the network.
The system must also be economically sustainable and ensure that
the distribution of rewards does not create incentives to exploit the system
or reduce the quality of the aggregated results.

Collusion attacks pose a significant risk to the system. A collusion attack
occurs when multiple participants, such as inference providers, coordinate
their actions to manipulate the aggregation algorithm, reputations, and reward
distribution. For example, providers could submit similar inaccurate
predictions to make their results appear correct and receive rewards. This is
difficult to detect when there is no ground truth and when the colluding
providers make up a large portion of the network.

\subsection{POC Demonstration Plan}

The proof of concept plan is to create a simulation of the DAIIM network that
is completely off-chain. The simulation will execute a sequence of rounds in which
an inference task for a regression problem is executed by the network. Several types of actors will participate in the network:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Honest: attempt to maximize rewards by providing accurate predictions consistently (with some noise);
    \item Learners: gradually improve their predictions over time;
    \item Malicious: intentionally provide bad predictions;
    \item Colluding: a group of providers working together to skew the network.
\end{enumerate}

The simulation will start with a simple Machine Learning (ML) task (e.g., predict $y$, where $y = mx + b$), and each round some input data will be randomly generated.
The different actors will be given some algorithm (good or bad algorithm depending on the actor) to determine their predictions given the input.
The actors will submit their predictions, and the aggregation algorithm will determine the aggregated result. Actors' reputations will be updated
and rewards will be distributed after each round.

Another layer of this simulation is to economically model the system. We will
assign providers a cost for running their model, transaction fees for submitting
predictions on-chain, and economic rewards or penalties for participating. These
values will be calibrated relative to the costs and payments that we expect a
provider to realistically encounter when performing an inference task. This
will ensure that the simulation reflects realistic participation decisions
rather than relying on arbitrary values. We will also vary these values to
measure how sensitive participation and network performance are to changes in
costs, fees, rewards, and penalties.

We will test various combinations of actors to evaluate the limits of the
system. These scenarios will vary the proportions of different actors, as well as the number of providers in the
network. These results will allow us to identify conditions under which the system remains reliable and economically
viable, as well as conditions under which malicious or colluding providers can
significantly influence the network.

\subsection{Consequences of Failure}
If the proof of concept fails and one or more of the identified risks are not shown to be sufficiently
mitigated, this would indicate that the current system is not able to provide accurate
aggregated predictions, is not economically attractive to participants or is vulnerable to collusion attacks.
This would require the team to re-evaluate the scope of the project and/or the design of the system.
If the proof of concept is unsuccessful due to a lack of developer resources and/or technical expertise,
then the team will meet with the supervisor to discuss shrinking the scope. Otherwise, if the cause of
failure is due to poor system design then the team will meet with the supervisor to
discuss alternative approaches to consensus, incentive design, and aggregation algorithms. 

\subsection{Success Criteria and Failure Response}

This proof of concept will be successful and show that the stated risks can be overcome if:

{\begin{itemize}
    \item The system provides accurate aggregated results that improve over time even with some limited number of malicious actors. We define accurate as the aggregated result being within a certain threshold (roughly 90\% of aggregated predictions match the ground truth). For this POC, we assume that the aggregated result will improve even with up to 10\% malicious actors in the network. The exact threshold for these percentages will be determined during the POC implementation and testing.
    \item It is more profitable to act honestly and attempt to provide good predictions than to be malicious. Good predictions are those that are close to the ground truth (low relative error < 10\%). These good predictions should be rewarded with a non-zero token amount. The exact threshold for this percentage will be determined during the POC implementation and testing.
    \item The network can be cold-started with the assumption that initially there are a majority of honest providers.
\end{itemize}}

If the proof of concept is not able to demonstrate that these risks can be addressed then the project's assumptions and scope
may need to be changed. Before proceeding to full
implementation, the team will review the findings with the supervisor and
decide whether to revise the design, broaden the threat
conditions, or reconsider the project's feasibility.

\section{Expected Technology}

This section describes the technologies that are expected to support the
implementation of DAIIM. Note that these choices are preliminary and subject to change later in the project.

\subsection{Core Implementation Stack}

DAIIM will use Solana as the blockchain network for recording transactions and
executing on-chain logic. During development, the team will use Solana Devnet,
a separate network for testing programs and transactions without affecting
Solana Mainnet. We will request free test SOL from a Devnet faucet to pay for
transaction fees. Since Devnet tokens have no real-world value, the team can
develop and test DAIIM without using real money before deploying to Mainnet.

Solana programs, or smart contracts, will be written in Rust. The conventional
off-chain backend will use Node.js with TypeScript and PostgreSQL for data
storage. The user-facing interface will be developed with React.js and Vite. 
Python may also be used to write simple machine learning models for integration
testing purposes.

\subsection{Libraries and External Components}

The expected external libraries and components are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textit{[Anchor]}: A development framework for building, deploying and testing Solana programs in Rust.
  \item \textit{[MetaMask]}: Used for accessing crypto wallets \& signing transactions.
  \item \textit{[Metaplex]}: Protocol and SDK for creating NFTs.
  \item \textit{[@solana/web3.js]}: Library for interacting with the Solana blockchain from JavaScript.
  \item \textit{[@solana/spl-token]}: JavaScript library for working with SPL tokens on Solana.
  \item \textit{[Vitest]}: Testing framework for the React frontend and other TypeScript code.
  \item \textit{[Solana Devnet JSON-RPC]}: Public RPC access for testing blockchain transactions and programs.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Version Control and CI/CD}
Git and GitHub will be used for version control and the project repository. GitHub Projects will
organize issues and track their progress. LaTeX will be used for the
project's technical documentation.

GitHub Actions will compile the LaTeX documentation automatically when relevant
changes are pushed or proposed. Later, the CI pipeline will be extended to
build and run tests for the Solana programs (using Anchor) and React frontend.

\section*{Appendix --- Reflection}

\input{../Reflection.text}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Why is it important to create a development plan prior to starting the project?}

    \indent Creating a development plan prior to starting the project helps to encourage thoughtful communication between team members and facilitates helpful discussions regarding expectations for the individual and for the group.

    \indent It helped to ensure that we were all on the same page and helped us to think about the future of the project. Outlining the Proof of Concept plan helped us to confront what is reasonable within the scope of the project, and we were able to identify possible pain points. For example, we realized that the UI will most likely be a large part of the project, but is not necessary to be super involved. In contrast, we found that we are still shaky on how we will implement the reputation system. Thus, this helped us to narrow our focus and identify the expected pain points of the initial development of the system.

    \indent Outlining roles and assigning actual metrics to the expectations really helped with administrative organization and getting a better understanding of how we want to work both as a group and individually. For example, conflict resolution was not something we had considered. However, we have now developed a system that will hopefully help in catching issues early, hold everyone accountable, and address the issues in a respectful, agreed-upon manner. It also helped us to think about the necessity of having a plan for consistent contributions and to have deadlines throughout the project to keep the team on track and avoid major crams.

    \indent While we understand that the information laid out in this development plan may not end up going completely as expected, creating this document led to fruitful discussions and agreements, and helped us to get a better understanding of the project as a whole.

   
    \item \textbf{In your opinion, what are the advantages and disadvantages of using CI/CD?}

    \indent CI/CD can be extremely helpful, especially in group projects, when executed correctly. It can help to outline certain standards. For example, having a set of rules that must be adhered to in order for a pull request to be accepted.

    \indent It can also help to prevent bugs from unknowingly arising when changes are made to existing code and disrupting code that was previously written off as meeting requirements and passing test cases. This is especially useful in a project with many collaborators and on complex projects, as small bugs introduced can be hard to catch and to pinpoint when they were introduced.

    \indent However, CI/CD can be complicated to implement and can add to project overhead.

    \indent As a group, we have decided to implement guidelines that must be followed when merging a branch into Main (no conflicts, at least one reviewer), the existing automatic PDF generator (with potential modifications as the project progresses), and the addition of unit testing where deemed necessary. We may choose to add more CI/CD elements to the project in the future, at the discretion of the group.

    \item \textbf{What disagreements did your group have in this deliverable, if any, and how did you resolve them?}

    \indent There were no disagreements in this deliverable. Everyone was respectful in sharing their ideas and listening to others. Any misunderstandings that were voiced were met with non-judgmental education, and agreements were decided from there. For example, there was uncertainty regarding the feasibility and expectations for the Proof of Concept. This led to educational discussions, constructive criticism, and educational moments.

    \indent The team navigated these discussions with respect and open-mindedness, and we believe this bodes well for the future of the project, conflict resolution, and the team dynamic.
\end{enumerate}

For this Capstone, our team hopes to deliver a project that is both challenging and enjoyable to create. We aim to build something beyond simply fulfilling the requirements of a school project---something that we can be proud of and potentially carry forward into our professional careers. By focusing on producing a meaningful, well-developed, and high-quality project, we believe that a strong performance in the Capstone course will naturally follow.

\section*{Attendance}

This section summarizes the ground rules and expectations regarding team member attendance.

\subsection*{Expectations}

All team members are expected to attend weekly team meetings and meetings with our supervisor. Attendance at every Capstone course lecture is not required, provided that members remain up to date with the course material through either attending the lecture in person or reviewing the material independently.

Attendance at all required team and supervisor meetings will be tracked through GitHub Issues. All members are expected to maintain a 95\% attendance rate throughout the project.

When a meeting is held in person, team members should make their best effort to attend in person. If a member is unable to attend in person, they should notify the team at least 5 hours in advance and attend virtually instead. If a member is unable to attend the meeting altogether, they must notify the team as soon as possible, preferably when they become aware of the conflict and at least 24 hours before the meeting when reasonably possible. Different expectations may apply depending on the circumstances.

\subsection*{Acceptable Excuse}

Team members are expected to communicate with the rest of the team if they are unable to attend a meeting or meet a project deadline. Each member may miss up to three team meetings per term with prior notice. Acceptable reasons may include illness, family emergencies, religious observances, or previously arranged commitments that cannot reasonably be rescheduled.

If a member exceeds three absences in a term, the team will discuss the member's participation, expectations, and responsibilities. If the issue cannot be resolved within the team, the team may involve the course professor or supervisor.

Team members are expected to meet all assigned deadlines. Exceptions will generally only be made in the event of an emergency, as outlined in the following section.

\subsection*{In Case of Emergency}

If a team member is unable to meet a deadline or complete their assigned work due to an emergency, they must communicate this to the team as soon as they become aware that the work cannot be completed on time.

The team will then discuss appropriate accommodations and, if necessary, redistribute the affected work to ensure that project deadlines can still be met. If another team member must take on the incomplete work, the original team member will be expected to take on additional responsibilities during a subsequent phase, where appropriate, to help maintain a fair distribution of workload.

\subsection*{Accountability and Teamwork}

\subsubsection*{Quality} 

To reach our goal of using this capstone experience to build something that we as a team are proud of, all members agree to make contributions that meet or exceed expectations. Level 4 of the rubric of deliverables will be referenced and used as a guideline for minimum expected requirements.

Team members are also expected to come to meetings with their assigned tasks completed and prepared to share their progress. Members are expected to actively contribute to team discussion and provide constructive criticism to help augment the quality of the project.

During code reviews, reviewers are expected to provide at least one point of constructive criticism to encourage consistent improvement.

\subsubsection*{Attitude}

All team members will contribute to creating a welcoming and non-judgmental environment in which members can feel comfortable openly sharing ideas. All ideas will be considered in their entirety and seen as valuable contributions to the development process, whether they are accepted or viewed as opportunities to provide constructive criticism and identify miscommunications or misunderstandings.

Members are expected to communicate respectfully and refrain from interrupting or speaking over others. Everyone should be actively listening during meetings and provide thoughtful insight to team discussions.

All members will communicate respectfully and professionally. Contributions will be acknowledged and openly appreciated. Cooperation is expected between team members and can be showcased through a willingness to learn, to compromise, to help fellow members when reasonably possible, and to give useful feedback during code reviews.

All disagreements are expected to be handled professionally and constructively. Should conflict arise, members will take a reasonable break until they feel that they can address it in a productive and professional way. If conflict arises that cannot be resolved by the team, a meeting with the TA and professor will be requested, and the entire team is expected to attend, even if certain members are not directly involved.

\subsubsection*{Stay on Track}

To ensure deadlines are consistently met and the project stays on track, a \href{https://github.com/users/robertLam04/projects/5}{GitHub kanban project} will be used and maintained to stay up to date on all issues and milestones/deliverables. All issues will be labelled with the type, the associated milestone, the assignee(s), and the target deadline for the issue to be completed and ready for review.

Deadlines will be assigned to each issue to encourage incremental, iterative, and consistent progress, rather than big pushes before externally enforced deliverable deadlines. The schedule and availability of members will be taken into consideration when assigning tasks and deadlines, to ensure that tasks and timelines are manageable and do not cause unnecessary stress. Tasks will also be assigned with a goal of sharing an even workload amongst all team members (1/4 of the expected workload). Team members will be free to voice their concerns if the workload does not seem even, and the task assignments will be reviewed and reassessed.

Members should update the status of their issues regularly as the status changes. When an issue is ready for review, the assignee will create a pull request and assign a primary reviewer. The primary reviewer must respond and give feedback within 48 hours. Team member contributions will be assessed based on Git activity such as commits, pull requests, and issues completed to show consistent contributions that meet quality standards, as well as meeting the planned deadlines.

At weekly team meetings, members will be expected to share the status of what they have been working on that week, as well as action items for what they intend to work on or complete before the next meeting. Each member should have an attendance record of $>95\%$. If consistent lateness becomes an issue, it will be addressed by the team.

In the case of the anticipation of a missed deadline, members should raise concerns about meeting deadlines at least 48 hours before the deadline to ensure the team can adjust and plan for the work to still be completed. In the case of a member needing to offload their assigned tasks onto other members to meet the deadline, it will be handled as a missed deadline and will be handled according to what has been outlined above in \textbf{Attendance}.

Members who are not meeting expectations will be respectfully confronted at the following team meeting. They will have the opportunity to explain their situation and must come up with a detailed plan describing how they will get back on track. This plan will be reviewed and approved by the other team members, and their progress will be reviewed at each subsequent team meeting until the member consistently meets expectations. If at the following meetings it becomes clear that little to no effort is being made to get back on track, the issues will be brought to the attention of the TA in an effort to seek external support in conflict resolution.

Members who consistently meet or exceed expectations will be recognized by the team for their contributions. This may include informal recognition during team meetings, opportunities to take on preferred tasks, or small team rewards after completing major milestones. If the entire team reaches a milestone ahead of schedule while maintaining the expected quality of work, the team may use the additional time for a team social activity or other agreed-upon reward.

\subsubsection*{Team Building}

Team building activities will be planned and hosted biweekly. Potential events may include, but are not limited to, team lunches and trivia nights. The events will be planned for a time that works for all members' schedules and will be mindfully inclusive of the needs and interests of all team members.

\subsubsection*{Decision Making} 

Team decisions will occur through consensus, first allowing all team members to voice their opinions and concerns. The team will collectively come to an agreement, with the success of the team and project as a whole as a priority.

In the case of a disagreement, each member will be treated with respect and given the opportunity to express their opinions honestly and respectfully. Efforts will be made to come to a healthy compromise.

If time constraints allow, any disagreement that cannot be resolved within the meeting will be revisited at a subsequent meeting. All members of the team will take time between meetings to view the situation from a different perspective to encourage empathy in the decision-making process and academic research, if necessary.
