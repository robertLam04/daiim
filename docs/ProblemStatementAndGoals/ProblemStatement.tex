\section{Problem Statement}

\subsection{Problem}

Current AI inference markets are increasingly concentrated around a small number of large organizations~\cite{vipra2023market}.
As the artificial intelligence (AI) industry continues to grow, we become increasingly reliant on centralized providers for AI models, data, hardware, and compute.
This centralization increases the risk of biased outputs because of the lack of diversity in training algorithms and data used by these companies~\cite{rolf2021representation}.
Furthermore, the oligopilized nature of the AI market creates a risk of censorship and manipulation of AI outputs. The lack of transparency from these companies and the black-box nature of many AI models makes it difficult for consumers to verify the quality and reliability of their inferences.
Lastly, the centralized nature of the compute infrastructure used by these companies creates potential single points of failure. According to the 2026 Stanford AI Index, NVIDIA accounts for over 60\% of total global AI compute capacity~\cite{aiindex2026}. 
This concentration poses a significant risk of disruption to AI markets in the event of a single failure. 
\newline

Our project is called DAIIM: Decentralized Artificial Intelligience Inference Marketplace.
Our aim is to provide consumers with accurate AI predictions through a decentralized network
of providers, reducing reliance on centralized solutions.

\subsection{Inputs and Outputs}

The inputs to the system consists of task definitions, inference requests, and network participation.
Task definitions are specification of a problem to be solved via machine learning, including the expected input and output types.
Inference requests ask the network to perform inference for a given task and include
input data or some specification of where to find the input data.
Network participation includes machine learning 
predictions for individual inference requests that are supplied to the network by an individual.
\\\\
The system outputs aggregated inference results and maintains information about the network.
The aggregated inference results include the final prediction for a given inference request, as well as information about the quality of the prediction.

\subsection{Stakeholders}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Task requesters} \newline
    Task requesters are people who are requesting inferences to be made for a given task. These people would value the decentralized nature of our platform. 
    For example, healthcare researchers would make requests to predict various regression inferences (such as biomarker levels) while still complying with privacy constraints. 
    Similarly, decentralized finance platforms could make requests to predict various regression inferences (such as stock prices) while still complying with privacy constraints.

    \item \textbf{Inference providers} \newline
    Inference providers are people with computing resources and models, and want to monetize their regression models.
    These people usually have specialized or powerful models that can be used to make predictions for a given task.
   
    \item \textbf{Data Providers}   \newline
    Data providers are people with data that can be used to train models for a given task.
    They would be interested in helping the inference providers improve their models by providing them with data to train on.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Environment}

This software is compatible with any computer running a modern browser that is connected to wifi. There is no hardware component. 

\section{Goals}

This project has a total of five primary goals.

\subsection{Automated and Decentralized Marketplace Transactions}

Enable users and model providers to autonomously establish and fulfill
marketplace agreements without requiring centralized coordination. This
goal will be measured by whether the system can successfully complete an
agreement from the initial request through payment and settlement without
manual intervention.

\subsection{Web3 Usability and Transparency}

Provide a user-friendly Web3 experience that abstracts unnecessary
blockchain complexity while maintaining transparency into the actions and
transactions performed by the system. The application should clearly
communicate the purpose and consequences of actions requiring user
authorization, including the transactions and data being signed, while
providing users with direct visibility into the corresponding on-chain
transactions, their status, and relevant state changes. This goal will be
measured by whether users can understand the actions they are authorizing,
successfully complete required Web3 interactions without directly
managing the underlying blockchain infrastructure, and verify the
resulting transactions and state changes through the application.


\subsection{Reliable AI Inference}

Enable users to submit AI inference requests and reliably receive results
from participating model providers. The system should ensure that
requests are successfully delivered, processed, and returned to the
consumer while minimizing failed, lost, or incomplete inference
requests. This goal will be measured by whether submitted inference
requests consistently reach an available model provider, are successfully
processed, and return a corresponding inference result to the consumer.

\subsection{Accessible Consumer Experience}

Provide a consumer-facing experience that allows users to interact with
the marketplace without requiring knowledge of decentralized systems or
their underlying infrastructure. This goal will be measured by whether a
user can discover a model, submit an inference request, and receive its
result entirely through the consumer interface.

\subsection{Model Provider Innovation}

Encourage independent model providers to participate and innovate within
the marketplace by providing opportunities to make their models available
to consumers, compete for inference requests, and receive compensation
for their services. The marketplace should create incentives for
providers to improve the quality, performance, availability, and
cost-effectiveness of their models, as models that provide greater value
to consumers can attract more inference requests and generate greater
potential revenue. This goal will be measured by whether a model provider
can register a model, make it available to consumers, receive inference
requests, execute the inference, and receive compensation for
successfully returning results through the system.

\section{Stretch Goals}

This project has a total of two stretch goals, which outline additional
features that could be implemented after the primary goals have been
achieved.

\subsection{Expanded AI Model and Inference Support}

Extend the marketplace beyond linear regression to support additional AI
models and more complex inference tasks. This is considered a stretch
goal because supporting multiple model architectures and more complex
inference workflows would require additional development beyond the core
marketplace functionality established by the primary goals. The
achievement of this goal would be measured by whether the system can
successfully register, discover, and execute inference requests for
multiple AI model types beyond linear regression.

\subsection{Private AI Inference}

Enable inference on private or sensitive data while protecting that data
from unauthorized access during execution through the use of Trusted
Execution Environments (TEEs). This is considered a stretch goal because
incorporating TEEs would introduce additional security, infrastructure,
and integration requirements beyond those necessary to support the core
marketplace and inference functionality. The achievement of this goal
would be measured by whether the system can successfully execute an
inference request on private data within a TEE while preventing
unauthorized access to the data during execution.

\section{Custom Documents (CDs)}

\subsection{Cognitive Walkthrough}

A cognitive walkthrough will be performed using a wireframe prototype of the 
planned interface of the application from the point of view of the inference requesters. 
This will aid in mapping the entire process of DAIMM and generally smooth out any remaining 
ambiguity. The results of the cognitive walkthrough will provide essential insight into the 
required user feedback and how to make the UI accessible and learnable for the expected users 
to ensure the application is simple to understand and enjoyable to use. 

\subsection{Business Plan}

A business plan will be developed, once a proof of concept has met expectations. The goal 
of the business plan is to detail how the decentralized AI marketplace can be implemented 
such that developers and stakeholders can be compensated, and will further explain the 
business strategy behind the tokenomics. The business plan would include a description
of the problem and the market need, the market size and business model, the cost breakdown,
the competition and positioning, and the execution plan and milestones.

\section*{Appendix --- Reflection}

\input{../Reflection.text}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item What went well while writing this deliverable?

    Our team was incredibly agreeable and had no trouble communicating our ideas for the project or our concerns about the scope. The team worked smoothly to accomplish the requirements for the deliverable. Everyone cooperated with one another, and it was easy to split up the workload evenly without disagreements or conflict. We were able to easily schedule multiple group meetings, which facilitated group discussions that gave everyone a deeper understanding of the project.

    \item What pain points did you experience during this deliverable, and how did you resolve them?

    There were varying levels of experience in the subject matter of ML, blockchains, inferences, and decentralized AI systems. Members who were less familiar with the content did some research on the subject matter, and members who were more experienced were extremely cooperative in answering questions in a non-judgmental and non-condescending manner. Notes of previous research performed by Robert were also shared as supplementary resources. Our team organized a couple of meetings to discuss the content in this document to ensure everyone was comfortable with the material and to encourage necessary discussion to determine the problem, users, goals, and scope of the project.

    \item How did you and your team adjust the scope of your goals to ensure they are suitable for a Capstone project (not overly ambitious but also of appropriate complexity for a senior design project)?

    Our team discussed our skills and experience, and ideas for features of the application. We considered the timeline and budget, and ultimately we think we can reasonably accomplish the project over the next few terms given our skill and experience levels.

    Initially, we brainstormed goals that we later deemed to be too ambitious for the scope of the project. For example, we had planned to try to implement multiple types of inferences. Upon further inspection, we realized that not only was this goal too solution-oriented, it was beyond what we could determine was reasonably feasible within the timeline of the project, considering the other features we are planning to implement. We instead included this as a stretch goal.
\end{enumerate}
